# Interview structure

The run is one structured interview, not a bag of question types. Each turn belongs to a phase, and the
arc is fixed by the depth dial, so two candidates at the same depth get the same interview shape.

| Phase | What it does | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rapport | One or two light, non-evaluative questions | Rapport is the first phase of evidence-based interview protocols, and it is what makes the rest of the answers usable |
| Background | Resume, then "tell me about yourself" | Grounds every later question in what the candidate actually claims, and gives the grader a yardstick |
| Competency | Past-behaviour questions, one theme each, each with a follow-up turn | Past behaviour beats hypotheticals, and structure (same questions, limited prompting, probes when the evidence is thin) is what lifts validity |
| Technical | Role-specific depth, correctness and the why | The job itself |
| Your questions | The candidate asks, the interviewer answers | Both public guides close on it, and a candidate who never gets to ask reads the silence as rejection |
| Close | Sign-off, no question | A question after the last answer leaves the candidate holding something they cannot answer |

Depth is the only lever: screen, standard, full loop. Competency and technical questions each own a
follow-up turn: missing evidence gets probed, and a complete answer gets a harder question about the
same story, so the dial's question counts are the counts and not a floor.

## Sources

- Schmidt & Hunter (1998), *The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology*:
  structured interviews predict job performance at .51, unstructured at .38.
  <https://pdf.stafforini.com/schmidt-1998-validity-and-utility.pdf>
- Levashina, Hartwell, Morgeson & Campion (2014), *The Structured Employment Interview*: structure is the
  deliberate, predetermined rules for questions, observations and evaluation.
  <https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12052>
- Canada School of Public Service, *Structured Interviewing*: introduce, ask questions, take notes, probe,
  then conclude and answer the candidate's questions.
  <https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/canada/public-service-commission/migration/plcy-pltq/guides/structured-structuree/rpt-eng.pdf>
- US OPM, *Structured Interview Guide*: closing on "is there anything else you would like us to know?"
  and giving the candidate the chance to ask.
  <https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/guide/>
- CREST, *The Cognitive Interview*: rapport and free recall before probing.
  <https://crestresearch.ac.uk/download/2245/16-006-01.pdf>
